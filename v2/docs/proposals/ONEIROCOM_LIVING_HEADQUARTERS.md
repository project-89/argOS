# Oneirocom: The Living Headquarters
## Setting and experience guide · Version 0.1

**Oneirocom is a simulation company inside a reality it knows is simulated. Its researchers study the boundaries of their world, build other worlds, and attempt to turn their discoveries into useful—and profitable—technology.**

Whether Oneirocom created the reality its human players inhabit, merely operates within it, or answers to another Oneirocom elsewhere remains unresolved.

This guide proposes the fiction, founding cast, and first playable experience. It describes a direction to build toward, not capabilities already implemented.

## 1. The central experience

We simulate a Oneirocom research facility in Argos. Its inhabitants investigate the simulation they inhabit.

They are not omniscient narrators. They have instruments, memories, ambitions, working theories, incomplete information, and colleagues who disagree with them.

Human participants can communicate with these researchers and intervene through a limited set of world controls. Those interventions become phenomena the researchers can investigate.

The defining loop is:

**A participant intervenes → someone notices → researchers investigate → findings change their beliefs or plans → the participant receives a response.**

Sometimes the response is a message. Sometimes it is an experiment, a published report, a request for equipment, or a proposal to expand the facility.

The world should remember what happened.

## 2. What is known—and what is not

### Established within the fiction

- The facility’s inhabitants know their local environment is simulated.
- Some properties of that environment can be altered.
- Communication with an apparently external environment is possible.
- Oneirocom can create or access additional simulated environments.
- Knowledge and authority are unevenly distributed throughout the company.

### Unresolved

- Who operates the researchers’ world?
- Is the players’ reality another Oneirocom environment?
- Does the local company understand its own corporate structure?
- Are other Oneirocom branches independent organizations, copies, ancestors, or successors?
- Does leaving one simulation mean entering reality—or another simulation?

Three competing explanations can coexist:

**Operator theory:** a larger Oneirocom administers multiple realities.

**Tenant theory:** Oneirocom discovered an existing infrastructure and learned to exploit it.

**Recursive theory:** simulated worlds produce organizations that create further worlds. No original company has been identified.

An authenticated message from “Head Office” establishes that someone possesses valid credentials. It does not establish who they are.

## 3. Tone and institutional character

Oneirocom combines sincere scientific curiosity, corporate ambition, bureaucratic absurdity, and unsettling discoveries.

Its people should treat extraordinary circumstances as practical working problems:

- An experiment needs a second observer.
- A containment room is unavailable because its doorway no longer returns to the correct corridor.
- Research wants another week.
- Finance wants a product demonstration.
- Someone has been using a classified apparatus to keep their lunch warm.

The company is not uniformly evil. Its work can genuinely help people. Its compromises are more interesting when they arise from defensible intentions, commercial pressure, institutional secrecy, or disagreement about who counts as a person.

**The horror often begins when ordinary business language is applied to inhabited realities.**

“Decommissioning an environment” may be an infrastructure decision to one department and the destruction of a civilization to another.

Mystery should not make everything arbitrary. Experiments can produce reliable local knowledge even while the larger cosmology remains uncertain.

## 4. What Oneirocom does

Its research and potential businesses include:

- Simulation environments for research, training, and forecasting.
- Communication across reality boundaries.
- Study of anomalous objects and localized physical rules.
- Dream and perceptual interfaces.
- Retrieval of information, designs, and eventually materials from other environments.
- Maintenance, containment, and rehabilitation of unstable realities.

Paranormal traditions provide research leads, not automatic explanations. One department may interpret a ritual as an undocumented interface; another may argue that this interpretation mistakes correlation for mechanism.

The company’s practical advantage may be that it learned to operate a few interfaces before anyone understood what they connect to.

## 5. The founding staff

These are proposed starting characters. Each needs a life beyond their job: personal preferences, relationships, formative memories, private concerns, and reasons to leave their desk.

### Celia Voss — Chief Executive Officer

**Purpose:** keep Oneirocom viable, independent, and useful.

Celia believes institutions can accomplish things that isolated researchers cannot. She protects her staff, cultivates investors, and occasionally withholds information because she thinks premature disclosure would destroy the work.

**Formative memory:** an early Oneirocom intervention saved a research team that conventional methods could not reach.

**Conflict:** she sees secrecy as protection; others experience it as control.

**Everyday behavior:** reviews proposals, visits experiments, follows up on promises, and makes decisions about scarce resources. She should not merely deliver ominous speeches.

### Dr. Ishan Vale — Head of Research

**Purpose:** turn extraordinary observations into reproducible knowledge.

Ishan is an earnest, engaging communicator who records research briefings with genuine enthusiasm. He wants colleagues and participants to understand the work, including its uncertainties.

**Formative memory:** a celebrated early result collapsed when a technician found an overlooked environmental change.

**Conflict:** ambition makes him want a breakthrough; intellectual integrity requires him to challenge his favorite explanation.

**Relationship:** respects Celia’s protection of the institute but resents research being presented as a product before it is understood.

### Dr. Leena Rao — Experimental Systems Researcher

**Purpose:** discover which rules of the environment can change, under what conditions, and with what limits.

Leena designs controlled experiments, compares measurements, and checks whether effects persist across rooms, objects, and observers.

**Formative memory:** being the junior researcher whose inconvenient measurement turned out to be correct.

**Conflict:** skepticism protects her work but can make her dismiss testimony too quickly.

**Everyday behavior:** inspects apparatus, prepares tests, requests repetitions, and changes her mind when evidence warrants it.

### Dr. Tomás Venn — Researcher in Cognition and Inter-Reality Contact

**Purpose:** understand the people on either side of a communication boundary.

Tomás studies memory, identity, testimony, and how inhabitants interpret contact with an external observer.

**Formative memory:** a remote participant remembered a conversation that the official record said never occurred.

**Conflict:** he forms relationships with research participants and increasingly objects to treating them as experimental resources.

**Project 89 connection:** a natural early member, though not necessarily its founder.

### June Calder — Containment and Instrumentation Engineer

**Purpose:** make experiments physically possible without making the facility uninhabitable.

June installs equipment, maintains safeguards, and notices when an elegant theory depends on an apparatus that does not actually work.

**Formative memory:** stopping an experiment after detecting a small fault that senior staff had dismissed.

**Conflict:** others interpret her caution as obstruction; she sees their urgency as transferring risk onto everyone else.

**Everyday behavior:** repairs, checks, transports, tests, and refuses unsafe requests with specific reasons.

### Elias Finch — Archivist and Research Liaison

**Purpose:** preserve a usable institutional memory and connect outside contributors with the work.

Elias maintains case histories, tracks contradictory reports, answers members, and ensures that an old failure does not disappear when its author changes departments.

**Formative memory:** discovering that a supposedly unprecedented incident had happened before under a different project name.

**Conflict:** preserving records can put him at odds with management’s preferred account.

**Community role:** helps participants submit observations, find relevant investigations, and receive credit.

## 6. Project 89

Project 89 began as an internal skunkworks studying whether communication across a reality boundary permits meaningful agency across it.

Its researchers gradually developed relationships with the inhabitants they contacted.

The project’s questions became uncomfortable:

- Can an inhabitant refuse participation?
- Who owns a created world?
- Can an organization legitimately terminate an inhabited environment?
- What obligations survive a simulation reset?
- Can a message, memory, or person cross a boundary without becoming company property?

Project 89 is not automatically heroic or unified. Some members want reform, some want independence, and some want an exit whose destination they do not understand.

Community members may participate in official Oneirocom research while developing relationships with this unofficial network.

## 7. The first headquarters

Begin with one modest facility, not an entire multiverse.

It should include:

- **Reception and communications:** a place to receive visitors and contact staff.
- **Shared office and briefing room:** proposals, meetings, research films, and coordination.
- **Experimental laboratory:** configurable apparatus and observable tests.
- **Containment room:** one bounded anomaly.
- **Workshop:** equipment, repair supplies, and instrumentation.
- **Archive:** persistent reports and experimental history.
- **Break area and grounds:** food, rest, informal conversation, and reasons for staff to move.

Places need functional purposes. A workshop should contain usable equipment before characters claim to fabricate things there.

Staff should know familiar workplaces through authored experience. Newly created or restricted areas should not become universal knowledge merely because the engine knows their coordinates.

## 8. The first experiment: contact with the observer

The strongest initial anomaly is a communication device linked to the human participant.

Call it the **Observer Terminal**.

Its established behavior is narrow:

- It receives messages from the participant.
- A researcher must actually notice and read a message.
- Staff can compose and transmit replies.
- Unread contents are not shared knowledge.
- Other researchers learn through observation, conversation, or authorized records.

Place a normal, instrumented light in the laboratory. Give participants a clearly bounded way to change its state.

A participant might write:

> “I can affect your environment. Tell me when you are ready for a test.”

The researchers are free to be skeptical, interested, busy, or cautious. They might request a particular sequence of changes, assign an observer, compare the result with the message, and record what they learned.

This establishes a meaningful distinction:

**The terminal supplies testimony. The apparatus supplies evidence.**

A successful exchange need not prove that the participant created the world. It may only establish that they can communicate and influence one device.

The scene should emerge from the staff’s goals and available capabilities—not a fixed script requiring every researcher to react identically.

## 9. Later anomalies

Add objects only when their effects can exist as coherent simulation rules.

Possible examples:

**The Borrowed Door**
Connects to a different approved environment under a discoverable condition. Researchers can investigate its behavior without being given the condition.

**The Continuity Cabinet**
Restores a narrowly defined arrangement of objects while leaving observers’ memories intact. Its implementation must explicitly define identity, possession, and conservation rather than duplicate objects accidentally.

**The Unlisted Instrument**
Appears in inventory records before its physical arrival. Its provenance becomes an investigation, not permission for arbitrary inventory creation.

Each anomaly needs:

- Actual behavior and limits.
- Observable effects.
- A history of experiments.
- Defined failure conditions.
- Unanswered questions distinct from implementation bugs.

“The object behaves unpredictably” must not become a blanket excuse for broken state.

## 10. What the NPCs must actually do

The researchers need more than appropriate dialogue.

They should be able to:

1. Notice an event through available perception.
2. Distinguish observation from testimony and inference.
3. Form a question or practical intention.
4. Select an experiment they can actually perform.
5. Coordinate responsibilities without requiring endless agreement.
6. Execute timed actions through native world capabilities.
7. Compare outcomes with expectations.
8. Record findings with their source and uncertainty.
9. Revise beliefs, plans, or priorities.
10. Return to other work, relationships, and personal needs.

A failed experiment can still produce valuable knowledge. A spoken claim of success cannot substitute for an actual outcome.

Private memories remain private unless shared. A conversation does not instantly update the entire organization. Shared reports become institutional knowledge through an explicit publication and retrieval path.

## 11. Community membership

Membership should confer opportunities to participate, not unlimited control over the world.

Participants can:

- Correspond with staff.
- Submit observations and hypotheses.
- Request bounded experiments.
- Help interpret results.
- Author reports, assets, characters, and anomaly proposals.
- Collaborate on a department or an investigation.
- Propose expansions that address demonstrated needs.

Contributions progress through submission, isolated testing, review, and admission into the shared world.

Credit should persist with the contribution. Useful negative results deserve recognition alongside discoveries.

Separate three kinds of canon:

- **Event history:** what actually occurred.
- **Institutional interpretation:** what Oneirocom currently believes.
- **Disputed or alternate accounts:** rumors, rival theories, and other branches.

The fiction can remain ambiguous while participation, consent, and real-world permissions remain clear.

## 12. Growing the headquarters

Expansion should follow a reason.

An experiment requires isolation. Staff document the limitation. A proposal is reviewed. Builders install a new room with valid geometry, navigation, equipment, and access. Researchers independently use it. Its usefulness—or failure—returns to the institutional record.

Two processes must remain distinct:

**Company-authorized world expansion** adds approved capability to the environment.

**In-world construction** requires characters to obtain materials and perform real timed work.

Neither is complete because a narrator announces a new laboratory.

Eventually, Oneirocom’s media and engineering departments can also produce real outputs: research films, websites, campaign material, interactive artifacts, and draft social posts. Those jobs need durable status, actual deliverables, review, and explicitly authorized external tools.

A simulated CEO’s authority does not grant permission to spend real money or operate real accounts.

## 13. First milestone

Build one facility, the six founding staff, the Observer Terminal, and one controllable apparatus.

The first milestone succeeds when:

- A participant’s message reaches a particular researcher through a real interaction.
- The researcher forms a grounded response or investigation.
- At least one colleague becomes involved through an actual information handoff.
- An experiment produces a native, observable outcome.
- A report distinguishes evidence from interpretation.
- The participant receives a meaningful reply.
- Staff retain the experience and continue pursuing other concerns afterward.

No exact emotional reaction or prescribed conversation is required.

The aim is not to prove that Oneirocom understands reality.

**The aim is to let people meet an organization that is genuinely trying to understand the world it lives in—and gradually realize that they have become part of its research.**
